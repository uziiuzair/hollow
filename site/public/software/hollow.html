Hollow by Uzair Hayat

Trace down where your space goes.

Hollow is a menu bar app for your Mac. It draws your whole disk as a map, so the big stuff is easy to spot, and it tells you what changed this week.

Download for Mac Source on GitHub

Version 0.1, 2.5 MB. macOS 14 or later. Free.

Why I built it

One app on my Mac had left 33 GB of crash reports in a folder I had never opened. About 180,000 of them. Nobody goes looking for that. You need something that puts it in front of you.

I wanted a faster alternative to Disk Inventory X, one that never freezes. So I built Hollow.

Double-click to go in

The window is a treemap on the left and a side list on the right. Double-click a block to open that folder. Right-click for Show in Finder and Move to Trash.

It works on the internal disk and on external drives.

It lives in the menu bar

A ring and the free-space number. No Dock icon.

Click it and the dropdown shows what changed on your disk: today, over 7 days, or over 30 days. So when the number drops, you can see why.

Nothing moves until you say so

Cleanup suggestions collect into a basket, and you go through them on a review sheet. Nothing is deleted outright. Items go to the Trash and you can put them back.

Hollow never empties the Trash. It tells you to empty it when you want the space back.

System folders are refused. Time Machine backups and system tools’ folders are not suggested.

It keeps up and it doesn’t hang

Hollow reads the disk once, then follows changes through FSEvents. The numbers stay current without rescanning, and a big new file shows up within seconds.

A folder that hangs, like a stuck cloud folder or an unanswered privacy prompt, gets skipped and retried. The app doesn’t freeze waiting for it. And it never triggers a cloud download.

Measured on my Mac, in an optimized build: about 1% CPU at idle with the map window open, and 1.4 s for a cleanup scan of a disk with 1.95 million folders.

It stays on your Mac

Hollow makes no network connections. I searched the source to check, and you can read it yourself.

It asks for Full Disk Access so it can measure Mail, Messages, Safari and some app data. It works without it. Those folders then show as unreadable.
